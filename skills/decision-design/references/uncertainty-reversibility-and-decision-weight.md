# Uncertainty, Reversibility, and Decision Weight

## Purpose

Scale decision effort to consequence rather than habit.

## Decision weight

Use four qualitative dimensions:
- reversibility: how cheaply can the choice be undone?
- downside: what happens if it is wrong?
- uncertainty: how much relevant uncertainty remains?
- blast radius: how many users/systems/teams/future decisions are affected?

High values on several dimensions justify stronger evidence and review. Low values justify speed.

## Reversibility classes

### Reversible / two-way
Use when rollback/substitution is cheap and bounded.
Default behavior: decide quickly, observe, correct.

### Partially reversible
Rollback exists but creates migration, compatibility, data, social, or opportunity cost.
Default behavior: identify rollback path before commitment and reduce the highest-risk uncertainty first.

### Effectively irreversible / one-way
Reversal is impossible, destructive, or disproportionately expensive.
Default behavior: strengthen evidence, alternatives analysis, authority check, and acceptance criteria before commitment.

## Uncertainty register

For a material decision, distinguish:
- known fact;
- assumption;
- uncertainty;
- preference/value;
- hard constraint.

An assumption that becomes decision-critical should either be verified, bounded by an experiment, or accepted explicitly as risk.

## Expected value of information

Do not research merely because more information exists. Seek additional evidence when it has a realistic chance of changing the decision enough to justify its cost/delay.

Useful questions:
- Which uncertainty could flip the preferred option?
- What is the cheapest credible way to reduce it?
- Would knowing the answer change action now?
- What is the cost of waiting?

## Anti-patterns

- heavyweight review for every reversible choice;
- calling a decision reversible when rollback would lose data/trust/compatibility;
- fake probability percentages unsupported by evidence;
- treating uncertainty as failure instead of a property to manage;
- refusing to decide until uncertainty reaches zero.
