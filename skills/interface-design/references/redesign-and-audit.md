# Redesign and Audit

## Establish incumbent truth

Before editing an existing interface, inspect the rendered surface and the code/design artifacts that currently define it. Missing documentation does not make the product greenfield.

Capture what must remain true:
- user-visible behavior and navigation;
- product facts and required copy;
- data/state semantics;
- accessibility/platform constraints;
- reusable components/tokens worth preserving;
- brand assets or explicit Creator decisions.

## Refinement vs redesign

**Refinement** improves hierarchy, spacing, typography, consistency, responsiveness, states, or polish while preserving the incumbent visual identity and product behavior outside scope.

**Redesign** may replace the visual language or structure when explicitly desired, while preserving product truth and functional requirements. Do not create a muddy midpoint by half-preserving a discarded direction.

## Audit order

1. Information architecture and task flow.
2. Visual hierarchy and density.
3. Interaction/state clarity.
4. Accessibility and responsive behavior.
5. Typography, spacing, alignment, color, iconography.
6. Motion and delight only after the fundamentals work.

Separate product/design findings from implementation defects when useful. Rank findings by user impact and scope rather than by visual novelty.

## Scope discipline

Fix issues that materially serve the requested surface. Do not use a redesign as permission for unrelated refactoring, copy invention, new claims, or product changes. Surface those separately when they deserve a Creator decision.
