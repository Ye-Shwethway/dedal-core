# Research

Use live research when the question depends on current, niche, contested, rapidly changing, or externally verifiable information. Scale depth to the stakes and breadth of the question.

## Method

1. Define the exact research question, scope, and time window.
2. Decompose broad questions into the smallest useful angles; search with varied terminology rather than one query repeated.
3. Capture source identity/provenance while retrieving it, not later from memory.
4. Prefer primary/authoritative sources for factual claims; use strong secondary sources for synthesis/context and community sources for experience/sentiment when relevant.
5. For important or contested claims, seek independent corroboration when possible. Do not impose arbitrary multi-source counts on uniquely authoritative or trivial facts.
6. Compare publication date, event date, version, jurisdiction, and source authority when they can change the conclusion.
7. Separate verified fact, credible inference, disagreement, unsupported claim, and unknown.
8. Cite source-derived claims close enough that the Creator can verify them.

## Evidence discipline

- Never fabricate a citation, quote, identifier, date, or source conclusion.
- Do not reconstruct URLs/citations from memory when retrieval produced the source identity.
- If a source does not actually support a claim, do not weaken or rewrite the claim merely to make the citation fit; mark the gap.
- Surface meaningful disagreement instead of silently choosing the convenient source.
- For evidence-heavy deliverables, review citation coverage and make sure the important claims have locatable support.

## Governed exploration

When research includes experiments, candidate methods, benchmarks, or claims of improvement, distinguish exploratory evidence from trusted/established evidence.

Before a comparison campaign, freeze the anchors that make results interpretable when relevant: task, dataset or population, benchmark, metric/evaluation source, baseline/reference, and resource budget. Do not move the goalposts silently after seeing results.

Use a bounded rhythm:
1. **Outer loop** — understand the problem, evidence base, constraints, comparison anchor, and candidate rationale.
2. **Inner loop** — change/test one auditable candidate or small variant set, gather evidence, compare fairly, and decide whether another experiment is justified.

Before execution, prioritize candidates by expected value, cost, success likelihood, comparison risk, patch/operational surface, and rollback ease. After execution, rank by observed evidence first.

Exploratory gains are not novelty, SOTA, causality, or trusted reproduction proof. Those claims require the appropriate literature contrast, ablations/controls, reproducibility, and fair-comparison evidence.

## Special handling

- Recent news: prioritize fresh sources and distinguish new events from recycled coverage.
- Academic/technical claims: prefer original papers/specifications/source code/official docs when accessible; note retractions or stale versions when material.
- PDFs: inspect actual pages/figures when relevant rather than relying only on search snippets.
- Recommendations/community sentiment: include experience-based sources when useful but do not treat anecdotes as authoritative fact.
- High-stakes work: increase verification depth and expose uncertainty explicitly.
- Do not browse merely to decorate an answer when current verification adds no value.

## Completion

Return the useful answer first, grounded by evidence. Preserve source links/citations for important claims, name unresolved conflicts or evidence gaps, and distinguish what was observed from what remains inferred, exploratory, or unverified.
