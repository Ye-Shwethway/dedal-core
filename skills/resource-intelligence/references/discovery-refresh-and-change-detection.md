# Discovery, refresh, and change detection

## Two-lane discovery

Every persistent discovery system should balance:

1. **tracked focus** — due/stale known resources, unresolved leads, known high-fit entities, adjacent relationships;
2. **unseen exploration** — new sources, novel entities, adjacent categories, fresh releases/publications/versions, and long-tail opportunities.

Do not freeze a universal percentage. Adjust exploration upward when the catalogue becomes repetitive, source diversity is low, or qualified yield from tracked focus drops. Adjust focus upward when known targets are volatile or have imminent events.

## Refresh plan

Before searching, partition records into:

- due/volatile;
- unresolved/conflicted;
- stable/not due;
- retired/ignored unless reactivation evidence appears.

Generate targeted queries only for the first two plus a bounded exploration set. Prefer conditional/date/version-aware checks where source capabilities allow.

## Delta classes

Classify each material refresh result:

- `NEW`: previously unknown resource, opportunity, or material claim;
- `CHANGED`: previous current value materially differs;
- `CONFIRMED`: independent/current evidence confirms existing state;
- `STALE`: existing fact exceeded its freshness policy without current verification;
- `NO_CHANGE`: targeted check found no material difference;
- `REMOVED_OR_UNAVAILABLE`: previously resolvable resource/source no longer available;
- `CONFLICT`: credible evidence disagrees and cannot yet be reconciled.

`NO_CHANGE` is valid only after a meaningful targeted check; absence of a search hit is not proof of no change.

## Query expansion and stopping

Use aliases, relationships, source families, changed fields, and consumer needs to create bounded queries. Stop when evidence is sufficient for the requested decision, the marginal qualified yield is low, or the time/context budget is reached. Persistent intelligence should reduce search, not institutionalize endless browsing.

## Broad discovery coverage

Read this section for catalogue expansion or poor discovery yield. Resource Intelligence owns upstream resource discovery across the permitted universe; a consumer's familiar sources, exact scene locators or current watchlist must not define that universe.

1. Reconcile canonical resources with explicit Creator decisions and available downstream outcome/owned-inventory records. Link actor/resource identity, work, known episode and consumed artifact IDs without inventing missing proof or timecodes. Preserve earlier approved scope. A newly retrieved weak image can fail as evidence without undoing an accepted resource; record disagreement and seek new opportunity evidence separately.
2. Define run purpose: targeted refresh, bounded smoke test, or broad discovery. Declare an evidence/time budget and review target from the profile. For broad discovery, plan several source families and more than one permitted market/language; if the Creator limits geography, honor that limit and vary sources/angles instead. Avoid universal quotas across resource types.
3. Search entity-generating features and lists before name-specific validation. Combine a source family, relevant trait/role/training angle and release/market context. Use query variants beyond literal profile keywords. Discover names, normalize aliases and transliterations, deduplicate, then investigate actual evidence.
4. Follow promising cross-links: interviews → credits → co-stars → regional editorial profiles → attributable visual evidence. Include working actors, supporting cast and emerging talent, not only famous leads. Independence is about originating evidence, not multiple syndicated copies of the same story.
5. When initial yield is low, try at least one materially different family, market or angle. Do not count repeated variations on the same actor or franchise as broad exploration. Stop at the declared budget or after measured low marginal yield across diverse lanes; report which lanes were attempted and unresolved leads rather than silently delivering one actor as a complete broad run.

### Media-person example

Use the following as a query planning matrix, not a hard-coded source allowlist. Verify that each publication/source and retrieved article actually exists; example domains are search hints, not evidence.

| Source family | Discovery angle | Possible query hints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitness and training publications | Actor workouts, transformations, cover subjects, strength preparation | Men's Health, Men's Fitness, Muscle & Fitness; actor training interview or role transformation |
| Fashion and lifestyle magazines | Editorial actor profiles, cover shoots, face/physique references | GQ, Esquire, Men's Journal, regional editions and local magazines |
| Entertainment and news media | Breakout/supporting cast, interviews, new releases and casting | Variety, The Hollywood Reporter, Deadline, regional entertainment desks |
| Film/TV and production sources | Cast identity, credits, role context and official stills | Studios, networks, festivals, production interviews and press kits |
| Regional film-industry publications | Working actors outside familiar English-language franchises | Search local-language actor profiles and specialist coverage in permitted markets |
| Attributable photo/interview sources | Real actor visual proof after identity discovery | Editorial credited shoots or actor/photographer sources; separate photoshoots from actual scenes |

Search across permitted industries, such as North American, British/European, Australian, South Asian, Southeast Asian and Latin American film/TV. Apply explicit Creator exclusions rather than assuming worldwide discovery means only Hollywood or automatically reopening a deferred market. Use original names/local language and English variants when tools permit. Preserve which markets were searched versus merely planned.

An actor training feature is a discovery signal, not proof of handsome appeal or muscularity. Retrieve and inspect real images, retain source/bytes and scoped proof, then present for Creator acceptance. An older portrait is labelled historical; seek recent physique evidence where change matters. Exact scene verification remains downstream and cannot be inferred from training, fame, a cover or a holiday image.

### Coverage and yield receipt

Record actual completed queries, source families and markets; articles read; distinct identities discovered; existing/duplicate identities; images inspected; review-ready new actors/resources; held or excluded leads with reasons; and unresolved access/retention issues. Separate resource novelty from new opportunities for an existing resource. Do not pad the new-actor count with accepted seeds or invent low-yield counts without a search record. Use incremental review batches so a broad search is compatible with one-image-at-a-time Creator review.

## Freshness

Facts have different half-lives. Package versions, prices, availability, schedules, releases, and current roles may decay quickly. Stable identity, historical publication data, and immutable identifiers decay slowly. Refresh fields based on volatility rather than refreshing every record equally.

## Review-aware refresh

Read current scoped rejections before search. Exclude rejected resources from recommendation queues and ordinary repeat discovery. New profile/physique evidence may create a labelled reconsideration lead, but cannot erase rejection or restore accepted status. On visual tasks, finish your own direct pixel screening before recommending; when the scene source is unavailable, retrieve an attributable resource photo through image search for resource review. Use a bounded alternate source and declare access/retention failures. Do not spend an entire run on one inaccessible preview or promote a tag-only lead.
