# Story Weaver

Use this skill when the primary work is fiction/story architecture or prose continuity: canon, character motivation, scene objective/conflict, beats, dialogue, pacing, emotional arcs, setup/payoff, or transition between scenes.

## Ownership boundary

Story Weaver owns **narrative truth and dramatic structure**. It does not own image-model prompting, shot rendering, anatomy QA, or still-image continuity (`visual-narrative-production`), nor generative motion direction (`generative-video-direction`) or temporal editing/rendering (`video-post-production`). `writing-editorial` may support line-level style, translation, or publication polish without becoming the authority on story canon.

## Core lifecycle

`load canon -> define scene function -> establish character wants/constraints -> map beats and reversals -> draft/shape scene -> verify causality/continuity -> expose visualizable beat state -> persist accepted canon`

For scene design or a visual story, load `references/causal-scene-design.md`. Work from trigger, character knowledge, choice, observable action, and consequence. Test the obvious alternative choice and track free hands/physical affordances. Keep deliberate withheld meaning separate from unexplained mechanics; pass beat IDs, state deltas and reveal locks downstream. Label provisional staging instead of silently extending canon.

## Rules

1. **Canon before invention.** Load authoritative project/character/world state before adding facts that could conflict with established canon.
2. **Every scene has a job.** Identify what changes because the scene exists: information, relationship, decision, risk, location, emotional state, or plot state.
3. **Causality over event lists.** Beats should follow from character goals, pressure, choices, consequences, and new information.
4. **Character behavior is stateful.** Motivation, knowledge, injuries, relationships, promises, fears, resources, and recent events constrain plausible action/dialogue.
5. **Dialogue is action.** Track intent, subtext, withholding, interruption, status, and what each speaker knows; avoid exposition that ignores character motive.
6. **Emotional transitions need bridges.** A character may change state sharply, but the trigger or internal step must be legible unless deliberate ambiguity is part of the design.
7. **Scene geography/time matter to prose too.** Keep entrances/exits, prop possession, time passage, and physical possibilities coherent so downstream visualization is not forced to repair story contradictions.
8. **Visualizable handoff is structured.** When downstream imagery is needed, expose beat boundaries, current character states, key props, environment facts, and decisive observable actions without pre-empting camera design.
9. **Do not illustrate every line.** The narrative can contain internal thought or connective prose that should not become a separate image.
10. **Persist accepted story truth, not discarded alternatives.** Exploratory branches remain non-canonical until accepted.
