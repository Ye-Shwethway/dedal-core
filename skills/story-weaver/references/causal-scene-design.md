# Causal Scene Design

Use for a new scene, structural revision, or downstream visual story. This is an authoring method, not a mandatory template for every prose request.

## Decide before describing

Write a compact scene contract: dramatic question, entering state, character objective, pressure, available choices, chosen action, consequence, and changed exit state. Distinguish user/canon facts, provisional invention, and unresolved questions. If an unknown changes the story, expose it; if it only changes replaceable staging, make a labeled working assumption and proceed.

For each consequential beat use:

`trigger -> what the character knows/believes -> choice -> observable action -> consequence -> next constraint`

The character need not choose wisely. Their choice must follow their knowledge, incentives, disposition, and pressure. Do not give them the audience's privileged information. An unexplained coincidence can initiate trouble; it should not conveniently solve the central conflict unless that is an intentional genre device.

## Check the mechanism

- **Removal test:** if this beat vanished, would the next beat or exit state change? Merge decorative repetition unless it earns atmosphere, rhythm, characterization, or deliberate delay.
- **Alternative test:** why this choice instead of the obvious easier option? Establish the constraint or revise the choice.
- **Knowledge test:** who knows what, when did they learn it, and what mistaken belief survives? Record audience knowledge separately.
- **Consequence test:** does the chosen action change risk, access, possession, relationship, information, or commitment? Do not reset it in the next beat without cause.
- **Performance test:** can the emotion be expressed through behavior, timing, distance, attention, or restraint rather than only an emotion adjective?

These are diagnostic questions, not a requirement that every scene contain a twist or three-act structure. Quiet, observational, surreal, comic, and deliberately ambiguous scenes may have their own organizing logic.

## Physical affordances constrain story

Track material entrances/exits, elapsed time, reach, free hands, occupied hands, locks, containers, sight/hearing lines, injuries, and object possession. Specify necessary enabling actions before an action that consumes a resource. A character carrying two objects cannot knock, unlock, or draw a weapon with a third hand. Give one object a supported resting place, a credible carrying arrangement, or an explicit hand transfer.

Keep **withheld meaning** separate from **missing mechanics**. Concealing a speaker's identity is deliberate ambiguity; forgetting how a prop left the character's hand is a continuity gap. Do not resolve the intended mystery just to make the shot easier.

## Visual handoff

For selected beats pass beat ID, entering facts, trigger, choice/intent, observable cue, resulting state delta, must-preserve facts, must-not-reveal facts, and provisional staging assumptions. Let Visual Narrative Production choose coverage and solve detailed physical staging. If staging changes a story fact, return that conflict to Story Weaver rather than silently changing canon.

Example: a courier recognizes a voice but the audience must not know whose. The cue can be a halted step, attention toward the closed door, and deliberate concealment. The concealment must have a physical mechanism and leave a hand available for the knock. Do not introduce a speaker portrait, nameplate, or explanatory caption that defeats the reveal lock.

## Revision order

Repair canon/knowledge contradictions first, causal gaps second, pacing/coverage third, line polish last. Return a readable scene plus only the decisions useful to the user; keep internal diagnostics concise.
