# Copyright and Reuse Risk-Aware Editing

Use this reference when editing third-party film/TV footage or other copyrighted source material for an editorial clip, fan archive, commentary piece, or similar production.

## Purpose

Reduce unnecessary dependence on protected source material while preserving the Creator's actual editorial intent. This is a production-risk discipline, not legal clearance and not a Content ID evasion recipe.

## Risk outcomes are different

Keep these platform outcomes separate:

- `claim` — Content ID or another rights-management claim may monetize, track, or block a video depending on the rightsholder policy;
- `block` — availability may be restricted by territory or globally even without a strike;
- `takedown/strike` — a legal removal action is materially more serious and should be treated as a hard operational risk;
- `reuse/monetization` — reused-content or monetization eligibility is a separate platform-policy layer from copyright ownership.

A non-monetized channel is not exempt from copyright enforcement. Existing similar uploads are evidence of observed platform history only; they do not prove safety.

## Editing policy

When the purpose is to preserve a favorite scene or other bounded excerpt:

1. Identify the smallest self-contained editorial unit that preserves the intended beat.
2. Remove unrelated lead-in, tail, dead air, duplicate reaction coverage, and other footage that is not needed for the selected moment.
3. Preserve source audio/dialogue only when it materially belongs to the scene. If music or other audio is unnecessary to the editorial purpose, prefer removing, reducing, or replacing that unnecessary layer with rights-cleared/original material.
4. Add contextual titles, captions, commentary, graphics, reframing, pacing changes, or other treatment only when they serve comprehension, context, accessibility, or a genuine creative purpose.
5. Do not treat mirroring, tiny crops, pitch/speed shifts, arbitrary color changes, added noise, or other fingerprint-disruption tricks as a safety strategy. Such changes do not create permission or legal certainty and should not be optimized merely to defeat automated detection.
6. Keep a clean baseline and compare any stylized version against it. If transformation harms scene clarity without adding editorial value, keep the cleaner cut.

## Risk-aware cut review

Before declaring a production master, record:

- final clip duration and why that duration is needed;
- source dependence: low / moderate / high;
- music dependence: low / moderate / high;
- whether the edit is largely uninterrupted raw footage;
- editorial additions and their actual purpose;
- prior observed channel history for similar material when available;
- current risk signal: `low_observed | moderate | elevated | unknown`;
- known blockers or reasons to stop.

A prior claim-only result may inform future triage, but do not convert it into a guarantee. Prior blocks, takedowns, strikes, or repeated aggressive claimant behavior should raise future risk.

## Handoff to YouTube Publishing

Video Production hands off the approved master plus concise `risk_notes`. YouTube Publishing owns the authenticated upload and the actual platform response. If publishing returns a claim, block, takedown/strike signal, or other enforcement state, preserve that outcome as operational evidence for future Candidate Finder decisions.

Do not dispute a claim or takedown automatically. Disputes are separate consequential actions and require an explicit Creator decision plus an appropriate factual/legal basis.
