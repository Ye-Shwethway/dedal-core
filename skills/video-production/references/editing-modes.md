# Editing Modes

Use this reference when the task is editing existing footage and the intended editorial logic is ambiguous. Video Production supports two distinct editing methods. Choose one explicitly before detailed cutting; do not accidentally mix their assumptions.

## Method A — Simple / Narrative Edit

Use when the Creator primarily wants the source scene, action, dialogue, or story beat preserved and cleaned up.

Primary objective: **preserve the meaning and readable progression of the source while removing unnecessary material.**

Typical characteristics:

- source chronology and causal order are normally preserved;
- dialogue, ambience, and source audio remain important unless explicitly replaced;
- entry/action/resolution beats define the useful editorial unit;
- reaction shots and context are kept when they improve comprehension or emotional landing;
- cuts should feel motivated by action, dialogue, gaze, or scene rhythm;
- transitions and effects are restrained and subordinate to continuity;
- captions/subtitles, audio cleanup, reframing, and delivery polish are applied after the structural cut is sound.

Recommended sequence:

1. define the requested scene/action/story unit;
2. locate and verify precise audiovisual boundaries;
3. build the clean chronological rough cut;
4. verify entry, comprehension, action/dialogue continuity, and resolution;
5. obtain approval when the Creator is iterating interactively;
6. add only the finishing work that improves the accepted cut;
7. verify the final render against the clean baseline.

## Method B — Fan / Visual-Montage Edit

Use when the primary goal is a subject-, mood-, performance-, fashion-, physique-, action-, romance-, or aesthetic-focused fan edit rather than faithful scene reproduction.

Primary objective: **maximize the intended visual/emotional motif while keeping the edit coherent, tasteful, and platform-ready.**

Typical characteristics:

- the target subject or motif, not full narrative coverage, determines shot value;
- source dialogue may be reduced or removed completely when it is not part of the creative brief;
- original or rights-cleared replacement music can become the timing spine;
- shot selection may span multiple moments inside the bounded source material;
- non-target reaction shots are minimized unless they improve rhythm, context, contrast, or emotional payoff;
- front, side, three-quarter, detail, action, silhouette, or other view types are selected according to the visual brief rather than merely because they occur first;
- chronology may be compressed, but visible causal/action order should remain sensible unless the montage intentionally signals a time jump or non-linear structure;
- a target-centric edit should usually open and close on the target subject or motif unless a different ending has a deliberate payoff;
- crop, subject occupancy, and border drift must be checked across the full moving shot, not from one representative frame.

### Fan-edit build sequence

1. **Lock the visual thesis.** State the target subject/motif and what counts as a strong versus weak view.
2. **Inventory candidate shots.** Scan the relevant source range and mark subject-dominant views, transitions, reactions, and low-value coverage.
3. **Build a structure-only rough.** Use hard cuts or minimal neutral transitions. Validate shot selection, order, framing, duration, subject dominance, and ending before adding decorative effects.
4. **Map music and rhythm.** If using replacement music, choose or create the track before detailed beat-sync polish. Preserve no source audio by accident when the brief is music-only.
5. **Bind accepted cuts.** Add transitions, punch-ins, retiming, blur, motion, grade, or other treatment only after the rough structure is accepted.
6. **Keep effects hierarchical.** Use effects to bind, emphasize, or redirect attention. Do not apply the same high-salience effect to every cut.
7. **Review crop through motion.** Check that the target does not drift into a border, become partially cut off, or lose the intended view during the shot.
8. **End intentionally.** Let the final target image or motion resolve rather than accidentally ending on a secondary reaction or transitional frame.
9. **Verify final output.** Compare against the accepted rough for sequence, target coverage, compression quality, audio replacement, transitions, duration, and delivery properties.

## Transition and effect discipline for fan edits

- Prefer hard cuts, match cuts, directional/whip transitions, short motion blur, subtle dip-to-dark, restrained zoom/pulse, or event-driven speed changes when they serve the rhythm.
- Repeated full-frame white flashes, strobing, or flicker are high-salience effects. Do not make them a default binding device; use only when explicitly justified by the style and inspect for viewer comfort/accessibility.
- A transition should hide or motivate a discontinuity, reinforce a beat, or create intentional energy. If it merely announces every cut, it is probably too strong.
- Treat speed ramps and frame interpolation as optional accents. Inspect cadence and interpolation artifacts rather than assuming they improve energy.
- Keep a clean structural rough as the comparison baseline so style can be removed without rebuilding the edit.

## Mode-selection rule

Choose **Simple / Narrative Edit** when scene meaning, chronology, dialogue, or action completeness is the main value.

Choose **Fan / Visual-Montage Edit** when a target subject, aesthetic motif, or emotional/visual hook is the main value and the source scene is raw material rather than the deliverable itself.

If both matter, declare a primary mode and borrow only the minimum useful rule from the other. Do not let a fan-edit effect pass destroy narrative orientation, and do not let narrative completeness dilute a deliberately subject-focused montage.

## Shared rules

Both methods still require source probing, exact media verification, copyright/reuse-risk awareness, a clean rough, deliberate re-encoding, final playback/read-back, and honest separation between technical render success and editorial acceptance.
