# Camera, Body, and Support-Equipment Alignment

## Purpose
Treat the performer, support apparatus, and camera as one geometric system. A body can have correct local anatomy while the scene is still physically wrong if the bench, platform, rail, machine, or other support is rotated or displaced relative to the action.

## Support-axis preflight
For any action where a support surface constrains technique, record when material:
- **body/action axis** — the torso or movement direction that defines the exercise;
- **support longitudinal axis** — the meaningful long direction of the bench/platform/rail;
- **required axis relation** — parallel, perpendicular, centered, offset, or another explicit relation;
- **contact map** — which hand, knee, foot, hip, back, or other surface contacts the support and where;
- **camera projection** — the chosen view that should reveal the action without changing the real 3D relationship.

Perspective may make parallel lines converge in the image, but it must not imply that the equipment is physically skewed relative to the performer when the exercise requires alignment.

## Camera solves visibility; the body does not pose for the camera
When a natural technique gives a profile or three-quarter profile, place the camera to see that profile. Do not twist the head, rotate the trunk, shift the support limb, or skew the bench merely to expose the face. Identity visibility is subordinate to action correctness.

For unilateral supported pulling patterns and analogous tasks, prefer:
- neutral cervical spine aligned with the torso;
- gaze naturally forward/down along the body/action line;
- support hand and support knee/foot placed where they produce a stable base;
- the working load traveling close to its intended path;
- bench/support axis physically coherent with the torso and support contacts.

## Admission failures
Reject or narrowly repair when:
- the subject is shown in a clean side profile but the support bench is visibly yawed/skewed into an incompatible 3D orientation;
- support hand/knee/foot contacts imply a different bench direction than the rendered bench;
- the camera-facing solution requires a head turn, torso twist, or support-equipment rotation that would be unnatural for the task;
- an otherwise correct pose is made physically implausible by support-surface placement.

## Generalization
This rule is not exercise-specific. Apply it to ladders, desks, beds, operating tables, workbenches, vehicles, rails, doors, ropes, or any support/constraint whose orientation materially determines plausible human action.
