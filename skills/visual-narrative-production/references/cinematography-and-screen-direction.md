# Cinematography and Screen Direction

## Camera follows narrative function
Choose shot size and viewpoint because they serve the beat:
- wide/establishing for geography, scale, isolation, threat, movement;
- medium for interaction and body language;
- close for performance/reaction/detail;
- insert for a story-critical object/action;
- low/high/overhead/POV only when viewpoint meaning or readability benefits.

## Shot specification
When material, record:
- framing / shot size;
- camera height and angle;
- subject-to-camera orientation;
- lens feel / perspective compression or expansion;
- camera-subject distance;
- depth of field;
- focal subject and eye line;
- negative-space intent;
- aspect ratio/orientation.

Do not use "cinematic 35mm" as a decorative default. Camera choices should solve a story/readability problem.

## Spatial continuity
For successive shots:
- preserve screen-left/right relationships when required;
- preserve movement and gaze direction;
- maintain or deliberately cross the camera axis;
- re-establish geography after a deliberate discontinuity or major relocation.

A still sequence can violate continuity just as a film sequence can.
