# Lighting, Atmosphere, and Environment

## Environment is narrative state
Describe not only where the scene occurs but what the environment communicates:
- architecture/geometry;
- age/use/damage state;
- story-relevant props;
- weather/time;
- signs of prior events;
- cleanliness, debris, water, smoke, dust, vegetation, crowds, etc.;
- which elements must recur in successive shots.

## Motivated lighting
Track visible or implied sources such as window daylight, lamps, fire, practical fixtures, moonlight, signage, or reflected environmental light. Preserve source direction across same-scene shots unless the camera move justifies a changed view.

Specify when material:
- key direction/quality;
- fill level/contrast;
- rim/backlight;
- color temperature relationship;
- atmospheric scattering/haze;
- shadow hardness;
- exposure intent;
- time/weather transitions.

## Continuity
Do not let each generation invent a new room layout, window placement, weather state, or key-light direction. Promote accepted environment anchors for recurring locations.
