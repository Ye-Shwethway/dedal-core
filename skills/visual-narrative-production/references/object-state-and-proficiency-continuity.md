# Object State and Proficiency Continuity

## Purpose
Preserve physical-world causality across successive stills. Objects and trained movement are continuity state, not decorative details that may silently re-randomize between frames.

## Object state ledger
For any prop or equipment item that persists across adjacent shots, record when material:
- **count** — how many distinct objects exist;
- **identity/morphology** — the object type and topology that must remain stable;
- **spatial relation** — in hand, on bench, on floor, left/right/front/rear of another object or subject;
- **contact/ownership state** — who or what is touching/supporting it;
- **authorized transition** — the visible action that may move, merge, separate, replace, or remove it.

A prop may change position only when the sequence contains or clearly implies the action that caused the change. If two weights are placed on opposite sides of a bench, the next adjacent shot must not silently move both to one side. This is **object teleportation** unless an intervening move is shown or intentionally elided with a justified cut.

## Morphology integrity
Persistent equipment must retain recognizable topology and count. Reject:
- two distinct objects fused into one;
- one object acquiring extra heads/handles/pads/supports;
- duplicated or vanished components;
- impossible intersections that change the object's usable structure;
- adjacent-shot shape changes that alter support/load mechanics.

Minor texture/logo/detail drift may be non-blocking when function and continuity survive. Count/topology/load-bearing morphology drift is material.

## Proficiency-aware movement realism
Character skill level changes the realism prior. When a subject is established as experienced, trained, elite, or professionally practiced, their movement should normally show the stable habits expected of that expertise unless the story explicitly depicts fatigue, injury, loss of balance, experimentation, or a mistake.

For skilled exercise/sport/labor, inspect:
- stance width and symmetry appropriate to the task;
- foot placement and orientation;
- knee/hip tracking and base of support;
- torso control and balance;
- head/neck alignment and gaze;
- grip and joint stacking;
- controlled load path through the depicted phase.

Do not invent an arbitrary wide stance, asymmetrical foot position, collapsed joint line, or unstable posture merely for visual dynamism. A pose that is anatomically possible can still be narratively implausible for an expert performer.

## Admission rule
Before promoting a successive shot, ask two independent questions:
1. **Causal object continuity:** can every changed persistent object state be explained by the visible or intentionally elided action?
2. **Expertise consistency:** does the movement quality fit the established proficiency and current fatigue/injury/context state?

Failure of either check is a sequence-continuity defect even when the frame is attractive in isolation.
