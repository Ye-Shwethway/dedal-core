# Performance, Expression, and Emotion

## Performance is embodied
An emotion label alone is underspecified. Direct the visible behavior:
- eyes/gaze target and focus;
- brow/eyelid tension;
- mouth/jaw state;
- head angle;
- shoulders/chest posture;
- hand tension/gesture;
- body weight and stance;
- breath/exertion cues;
- distance/orientation toward another character.

## Emotional anchors
For recurring characters, approved expression references may act as performance anchors while identity remains controlled by the canonical identity anchor. Useful states can include neutral, restrained anger, fear-alert, grief held back, tenderness, genuine amusement, exhaustion, combat focus, or other story-specific states.

Do not let an expression anchor silently redefine face shape, age, grooming, or physique.

## Inter-character performance
For dialogue/interaction shots, specify:
- who looks at whom or avoids eye contact;
- who occupies more/less frame space;
- distance and touch/contact;
- open vs closed posture;
- approach/withdrawal direction;
- whether the beat is cooperative, tense, intimate, suspicious, confrontational, etc.

Prefer observable behavior over melodramatic adjectives.

## Gaze follows action logic
Gaze is performance state, not an identity invariant. Derive it from what the character is doing:
- locomotion normally looks toward the path, destination, hazard, or relevant partner;
- manipulation/contact tasks normally look at the object or along the action line when attention is required;
- exertion may use neutral-forward or slightly target-oriented gaze according to biomechanics;
- deliberate look-away is used only when the narrative beat motivates it.

Do not copy a look-away pose from a face/reference sheet into an unrelated action just to expose facial features. Identity visibility must be solved with camera placement, not implausible head/gaze behavior.
