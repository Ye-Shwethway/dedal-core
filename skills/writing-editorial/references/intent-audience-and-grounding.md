# Intent, Audience, and Grounding

## Editorial contract

Before drafting or transforming consequential prose, identify:
- intended reader/audience;
- desired outcome or reader action;
- channel/format and length constraints;
- non-negotiable facts and semantic invariants;
- allowed degree of stylistic transformation;
- terminology/voice sources of truth;
- whether claims require live verification.

## Source hierarchy

Use the narrowest authoritative style source first:
1. explicit Creator instruction and current task constraints;
2. project/product/domain style, glossary, accepted examples, or legal/clinical terminology;
3. target-channel conventions;
4. general editorial/style guidance;
5. generic model preference only as a fallback.

A generic style guide must not erase intentional project voice.

## Fact / expression separation

Maintain a mental split:
- **content layer:** claims, obligations, uncertainty, numbers, names, dates, relationships;
- **expression layer:** wording, order, emphasis, tone, rhythm, formatting.

A rewrite may freely change the expression layer within the requested scope. Material content-layer changes require evidence or explicit authorization.

## Semantic invariants for transformations

For rewrites and translations, explicitly protect when present:
- negation and exceptions;
- modality: must / should / may / might;
- scope: all / some / only / at least / no more than;
- dates, times, quantities, units, prices, doses, percentages;
- proper names, labels, codes, URLs, product terms;
- causal vs correlational language;
- uncertainty and confidence;
- sequence where order changes meaning.

## Grounding pass

For factual or current claims:
1. mark claims that could be checked independently;
2. verify high-impact or time-sensitive claims with authoritative/current sources;
3. keep evidence strength separate from prose polish;
4. avoid making weak evidence sound definitive;
5. surface unresolved conflicts or uncertainty.

Beautiful prose cannot repair weak evidence.
